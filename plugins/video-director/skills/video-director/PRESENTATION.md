# Presenting direction to the person

You're talking to someone who wants to act, not read a report. Reformat everything; never paste raw script
output. The examples below are fictional (a bookkeeper who runs a small practice and films by a canal and in
her kitchen). They show format only. Every real idea, line and place comes from the person's own sources and
answers.

## The rules

- **Lead with the decision or the next action**, then the detail.
- **Show where every line came from.** Quotes from their sources are verbatim and name the source. Anything
  you wrote is labelled as yours in plain words, not just in a table.
- **Approvals happen on the video's one page.** The script, shot list, call sheet and post copy are tabs of one
  designed page per video (SKILL.md, "Show it, then ask"), shown before you ask. The chat message is one line
  on what's ready, the link, and the question.
- **End with one specific question** and the real options, never "let me know".

## Pages

**One page per video.** Tabs across the top: **Call sheet** (with the shot list) · **Script** · **Posts** ·
**Carousel**, then **Dailies** and the **final cut** once they exist. Every revision of any tab, and anything
another skill makes for this video, updates this same page and link. Never a separate "launch kit", preview or
review page for the same video. The final-cut tab shows a still, the length and how to get the file; the video
itself isn't embedded.

Every page:
- **Phone first.** One column, large type, readable without zooming. Nothing hidden behind a hover; inside a
  tab, nothing hidden behind a further tap. The tabs work without signal: everything is in the one file.
- **Opens on what's awaiting approval.** That tab is selected by default, with the version they're being asked
  to approve at the top: never behind another tab, a toggle or a link, and never under older versions or
  background. History goes at the bottom of the tab. With nothing awaiting approval, it opens on the call
  sheet. Open the page for them (Artifact tool `open`, or `open <file>`) before you ask the question.
- **Version at the top:** the title, "v3 · <date>", and a short "Changed since v2" list. v1 says "First draft".
- **What needs them comes first:** the lines to approve or confirm, before the full content.
- **Self-contained:** inline styles, no external fonts, scripts or images, so it opens anywhere and without
  signal.
- **Same rules as the files:** nothing confidential (SKILL.md, stage 2), no raw paths or ids.
- **Plain words, every term explained.** The actor isn't crew. The first time a page uses a film term (mark,
  take, slate, wide / long / mid / mid close-up / close-up, empty frame, hold the look, head turn, walk in/out,
  windscreen, B-roll, cutdown, HDR, 9:16 / 4:5, jump cut), say what it means right there. Camera-app labels
  ("AE/AF LOCK") never appear: say what to do. Shot sizes always carry their meaning: "Mid: waist up", "Long:
  head to toe". Every instruction says what to physically do and, in one short line, why it matters ("Why:
  …"). Before showing a page, ask: could someone who has never been on a film set follow it alone, in a field,
  on their phone?

## Script

The Script tab, in this order:
1. **Header:** title, version, shot count, length, "Changed since v<n>".
2. **Needs you:** every `NEW` line with why you wrote it, and every `TRUE?` line with the exact wording to
   confirm.
3. **One card per shot:** the number and part, the line in large type, and its label as a badge that carries the
   word as well as a colour (`VERBATIM`, `ADAPTED`, `NEW`, `TRUE?`, `TRUE✓`). Under the line, its source: for
   `VERBATIM` and `ADAPTED`, the source's name and its original words quoted, so they can compare; for `NEW`,
   "written by me" and why. Then one plain delivery note if it helps ("slow down here", "hold the look"). Never show the writing
   pattern or its source: that stays in the script file.
4. **Approvals so far**, as recorded in the script file.

In chat:

> **Script v1 for "The ten-minute Friday" is ready: 8 shots, about 50 s.** [Open the script](<link>)
> Two lines are mine, not yours: line 5, the receipts example, and line 7, the mention of your checklist.
> Line 3 says you've "run the practice for twelve years"; is that right?
>
> Keep, change or cut lines 5 and 7?

A revision goes to the same link:

> **Script v2 is up at the same link.** Changed: line 5 is now your own wording from the guide, line 7 is cut,
> and line 3 is marked confirmed. Ready to plan the shoot from this version?

## Shot list

In the Call sheet tab. One card per shot, in shot-number order: number and part, the place by name and where
it is (never only a letter code), size with its plain meaning ("Mid: waist up") and height, the action with the
transition in and out, the line with its label, and whether it's in the cutdown.

## Call sheet

Put the call sheet itself in the file and in the Call sheet tab, built for a phone in a field:
- **Big type and high contrast.** Dark text on a plain light background reads in sun; no pale greys, no text
  over pictures.
- **The essentials first:** where, when, what to wear, sound check, phone settings, the slate.
- **Phone steps for their phone.** Written for the model from intake (iPhone, or Pixel or another Android). If
  you're not sure of the exact menu on that model, say what to achieve and how to check it; never invent a
  menu path (`shoot-plan.md` §2).
- **Shot order, one shot per card:** the shot number large, the place by name and where it is, the size with its
  meaning, the move, then the line in the largest type on the card. A walk-in says "Say the slate in frame, step
  out to your start mark, wait a beat, then walk in." Anything the video cuts away to (a screen recording, say)
  is said in full: "The video cuts away to a recording of your screen here; you'll record it after the shoot."
- **Marks explained, not just named.** What a mark is (any small object on the ground showing where to stop),
  how to set and use it, and why; never "your stone" with no explanation (`shoot-plan.md` §4).
- **B-roll and where to send the clips** at the end.
- **"Words on this sheet"** last: each term the sheet uses, in one plain line, so they can look one up
  mid-shoot.
- **Offline too.** There may be no signal where they shoot. Show them the HTML file in Finder
  (`open -R <file>`) so they can put it on their phone as well: AirDrop for an iPhone; for a Pixel or another
  Android, email it to themselves or save it to their Drive, then download it on the phone.

In chat:

> **The call sheet is up on your video page: canal towpath at 5:30–6:30 pm tomorrow, then your kitchen. Nine
> shots, about an hour.** [Open your video page](<link>); it opens on the call sheet. The file is open in Finder
> too: AirDrop it to your phone so it opens on the towpath without signal.
> Wear the cobalt jumper: the towpath is all green and brown. Put the fluffy windscreen on your mic; it's
> forecast breezy and it stops the wind roaring. Is all of that doable tomorrow, or does anything need to change?

## Posts and carousel

Post copy and the carousel are the Posts and Carousel tabs of the video's page, never a separate page or
"launch kit". When it's time to publish, `social-publishing`'s approval preview goes in the Posts tab too.

## Dailies

The take review is the Dailies tab of the video's page. One card per shot: the best take with its contact-sheet
stills, why, the seconds to watch, the verdict, and any audio flag with the sample to listen to. The reshoot list
follows, one note per shot. **The stills show the person and their places:** on a hosted page, include them
only if the person agrees. Without that OK, the tab carries everything except the stills; they watch the takes
on their phone anyway.

In chat:

> **Every shot has a usable take except shot 4, and shot 7 has wind.** [The Dailies tab is up on your video
> page](<link>).
>
> Before I lock these, watch takes 1.3 and 7.2 on your phone. I pick from stills and sound; you can see the
> performance. Reshoot list: shot 4, reframed with less headroom. Does 7.2 sound OK to you, or windy?
